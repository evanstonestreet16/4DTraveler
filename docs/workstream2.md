# Workstream 2 — Core Experience / Navigation

This is the planning document for Workstream 2, one of the four parallel tracks defined in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) for work after Dummy v0. It exists alongside [PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md](PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md) and [DUMMY_V0_SPEC.md](DUMMY_V0_SPEC.md) as a living plan for this track specifically, in the same spirit as the Workstream 1 plan (`docs/3D_MODELING.md` on `codex/3d-modeling`).

## 1. Scope & Ownership

Per `AGENTS.md`, Workstream 2's deliverable is **a polished and understandable path from globe → location → era → world → POI**. It owns:

- `src/components/location/`, `src/components/timeline/`, and world-loading UI.
- Globe/location selection, era timeline, navigation hierarchy, loading/progress states, and route or deep-link behavior if introduced.
- `CameraController` behavior: easing, duration, interruption, reduced motion, return-to-overview, repeated POI transitions.
- Responsive layout and transitions around the world canvas (excluding object information and narration content).
- User-facing recovery when a world or model cannot load.

This plan adds two small new areas that fall naturally under the same ownership:

- A **globe subsystem** (replacing the card-based location picker).
- A **geo-data layer** (`src/data/geo/`) that powers the globe — real-world country and region boundaries, distinct from historical world content in `src/data/worlds/`.

Boundary reminder from `AGENTS.md`: consume camera presets and world data as given; do not edit GLB geometry, scene composition, object selection, or historical content — those belong to Workstreams 1, 3, and 4 respectively. In particular, **which cities exist as destinations is a content decision, not ours.** This plan describes how the globe surfaces whatever `src/data/locations.ts` contains; it does not add or remove destinations.

## 2. Current State (on `main`)

The globe's hover and pin layers are implemented and verified in a browser. Remaining state:

- `LocationSelector.tsx` — a globe stage with a logo intro that morphs into the globe after a fixed 2.2s delay (skipped on repeat visits via `sessionStorage`). The old card grid is gone.
- `GlobeView.tsx` — a `react-globe.gl` sphere with land/water fill, country hover outlines, and the city pins described in Section 3. All geo data is a synchronous lookup; the component makes no network calls, verified by observing zero external requests across a hover session.
- `src/data/geo/` — baked country outlines and city pins, generated by `npm run geo:build`.
- Verified end to end: hovering the US, Japan or Italy reveals their pins; a pin holds steady under the cursor rather than flickering out; clicking the Pittsburgh pin reaches the era selector with both the 1892 and 1850 eras.
- `EraSelector.tsx` — already generic: it maps over `location.eras`, so it needs **no rework** to support additional eras.
- `CameraController.tsx` — a fixed ~0.95s eased transition between camera views, respects `prefers-reduced-motion`, but has no loading-progress hook and assumes the destination view is already fully known.
- `App.tsx` — a `Suspense` boundary around `WorldExperience` with a static "Loading your historical world…" fallback, and a generic `.notice` block (with a single "choose again" link) for both "location unavailable" and "world not available yet" — no retry/recovery beyond starting over.
- No routing/deep-linking exists; navigation is purely `useReducer` state in `src/app/state.ts`.

Four defects found and fixed while building this. The first three are easy to reintroduce, so they are recorded rather than just closed:

- **Ring winding.** three-globe triangulates polygon caps assuming *clockwise* outer rings, matching the Natural Earth data it is normally fed. Counter-clockwise (RFC 7946) winding inverts every cap so it fills the whole sphere *except* the polygon. The symptom is the globe washing over in a single feature's fill colour. The bake normalises winding; see Section 4.1.
- **Cap curvature.** `polygonCapCurvatureResolution` had been raised to `180` to work around what was almost certainly the winding bug above. Coarse subdivision makes a large polygon's flat interior chord through the sphere: measured, `10` pits Canada and Nevada, `180` sinks the whole continental US below the water surface. Pinned at `5`.
- **Pins unmounting under the cursor.** Covered in Section 3 — pin visibility cannot be tied directly to polygon hover.
- **Coarse country outlines.** 1:110m was visibly blocky one zoom step in; replaced with 1:50m at 0.05°.

## 3. Landing Globe

Replaces `LocationSelector`'s internals (it keeps its role as the app's entry screen) with an interactive 3D globe, per the product direction: *"explore not only where the world is, but where the world was."*

**Intro sequence.** A `GlobeIntro` component shows a placeholder logo (a swappable image slot — real artwork arrives later) for a few seconds, then scales/fades into the full interactive globe. The morph triggers off a fixed delay for now, structured so the delay can later be swapped for "wait until globe assets have actually loaded" without changing the component's shape.

**Base globe.** A draggable, zoomable sphere showing only two tones — land (green) vs. water — with no country or city detail visible by default. Built with **`react-globe.gl`** (on `three-globe` + d3) rather than fully custom React Three Fiber: it provides drag/zoom controls, polygon layers, hover callbacks and label rendering out of the box, avoiding hand-built sphere raycasting and point-in-polygon logic. It renders its own Three.js scene, which is fine — the globe is a separate screen from the existing R3F `WorldCanvas`, never nested inside it.

**Hover interaction.** Hovering any country outlines it and labels it with the country
name. That is the only geography the globe draws.

**City pins.** Hovering a country also reveals its city pins, for the three countries that
have them: the United States, Japan and Italy. Pins are the globe's only click target.

Region outlines were built and then removed. They worked, but two datasets tracing the same
coastline — Natural Earth for countries, geoBoundaries for regions — do not align, so at
high zoom every coast showed mismatched slivers between the two. Pins have no such overlap
to get wrong, they make clickability explicit instead of implied, and a pin on Rome *is*
Rome, where a highlighted region only gestures at the city inside it. Dropping the region
layer also removed ~90 KB gzipped and the whole per-country admin-level problem.

**Pin behaviour.**

- Pins appear only while their country is hovered, and there are none for the other 239
  countries.
- Every pin looks the same. Clicking one backed by a curated `Location` enters it; clicking
  any other is a silent no-op, per the rule below.
- Pins persist until a *different* country is hovered, rather than clearing the moment the
  pointer leaves the country polygon. This is load-bearing, not a flourish: moving onto a
  pin clears the polygon hover that spawned it, so clearing pins on hover-out unmounts them
  from under the cursor and they can never be clicked.
- `pointRadius` is in degrees, so pins scale with zoom. It is set to 1.5 because that is
  what makes them reliably hittable — measured, at 0.28 only 2 of 11 US pins could be hit
  on a 14px pointer sweep, at 0.8 four, at 1.5 all of them.

**Click behaviour.** A pin is clickable when a curated `Location` claims it. The intended
destinations are **Rome** (Italy) and **Kyoto** (Japan); US pins exist to demonstrate the
capability and are not intended to become clickable. Today the only curated `Location` is
Pittsburgh, so its pin is the only one that does anything — which is also what keeps `main`
demoable and gives the Playwright suite a way into the world.

Clickability is **derived from the curated locations list**, never hardcoded to city names:
the globe asks "does a `Location` claim this pin?", so adding or removing a destination is a
content change in `src/data/locations.ts` with no globe code change. That keeps generic
globe components free of city-specific assumptions, per `AGENTS.md`.

**Contract.** `Location.globe` is `{ countryIsoA3, city }`, where `city` matches a pin in
`src/data/geo/cities.json`; coordinates come from the bake, so they are not duplicated in
content data. Per `AGENTS.md` this shared-contract change still needs a coordinated PR with
the integration owner.

## 4. Geo Data Plan

**Everything the globe draws is baked into the build. There are no runtime network calls, no
caches, no retries and no fallback paths.** The globe renders identically on every load,
offline, on a conference network, or on a laptop with no API keys.

Both layers come from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public domain) at
pinned revision `v5.1.2`, so a re-bake is reproducible. A single source for both layers is
deliberate — the mismatch that killed the region layer came from mixing two.

| File | Source layer | Contents | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `countries.json` | 1:50m admin-0 countries | 242 countries, 1608 polygon parts | 189 KB gzip |
| `cities.json` | 1:50m populated places | 31 pins across USA, JPN, ITA | ~1 KB gzip |

### 4.1 Bake pipeline

`scripts/build-geo.mjs`, exposed as `npm run geo:build`, following the existing
`scripts/*.mjs` + namespaced-npm-script convention used by `assets:hero` and
`assets:budget`. It:

- Simplifies country geometry with Douglas-Peucker at **0.05° tolerance** and rounds
  coordinates to **3 decimals**. Tolerance sets the crispness ceiling when zoomed in: one
  pixel is ~0.6° on a 600px whole-globe view but ~0.06° zoomed to a continent, which is why
  1:110m at 0.6° looked blocky one zoom step in.
- Drops polygon parts smaller than the tolerance — they cannot be represented faithfully at
  it and are sub-pixel on the globe. This is what keeps Alaska from contributing hundreds of
  meshes for the Aleutians.
- **Normalises ring winding to clockwise outer rings, counter-clockwise holes.**
  Non-negotiable; Section 2 has the detail.
- Slims properties to `{ name, isoA3 }` and `{ name, isoA3, lat, lng }`, using `ADM0_A3`
  and `ADMIN` for the reasons in `src/data/geo/README.md`.
- Picks pins as the ten largest cities by population per country, plus every city named in
  `CURATED_CITIES` regardless of rank — Pittsburgh ranks well outside the US top ten, and a
  curated `Location` with no pin is unreachable.
- Sorts by name so a re-bake is byte-identical, and fails loudly if a country count or a
  curated city goes missing.

Total geo payload is ~190 KB gzipped inside the already-lazy `GlobeView` chunk, which
carries `three` and `three-globe` and is ~649 KB gzipped in total.

## 5. Anticipating the Workstream 1 Merge

Workstream 1's `codex/3d-modeling` work is now on `main` (hero GLB, atmosphere/immersive rendering, the 1850 era). Remaining hardening for heavier scenes:

- `EraSelector` needs no changes — it is already data-driven over `location.eras`.
- `CameraController` and world-loading UX should be hardened for heavy GLB loads: replace the static "Loading your historical world…" text with real progress feedback (`@react-three/drei`'s `useProgress`, or R3F Suspense progress), and give `App.tsx`'s generic `.notice` blocks an actual retry affordance instead of only "choose again."
- Verify `CameraController`'s easing/interruption logic against camera presets and POI counts it has not seen yet (varied atmosphere-driven scenes). It should already be free of city-specific assumptions — treat this as a verification item, not a rewrite.

## 6. Conflict: two globes in flight

`feature/globe-history-generator` contains a **second, independent globe** under `src/components/globe/`, actively developed in parallel with this one. Both must not land.

|  | This plan (`src/components/location/globe/`) | `feature/globe-history-generator` (`src/components/globe/`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renderer | `react-globe.gl` (three-globe + d3) | Hand-built R3F sphere with its own lat/lon math |
| Geography | Country and region polygon layers | None — cities as points on a sphere |
| Purpose | Browse hand-authored worlds | Generate worlds on demand via Grok, models via Tripo |
| Data | Baked, offline, deterministic | Live API calls, `.env` keys, two Vite dev proxies for CORS |
| Entry screen | Rewrites `LocationSelector` internals | Rewrites `LocationSelector` *and* reroutes `App.tsx` |

Overlapping files: `LocationSelector.tsx`, `App.tsx`, `src/app/state.ts` (that branch adds a `mode: 'globe' | 'catalog'` concept), `src/styles/globals.css`, and `src/types/world.ts`.

Two points for the integration owner:

- **Both branches modify the shared contract `src/types/world.ts` without a coordinated contract PR.** This plan adds `Location.globe`; that branch widens `ScenePrimitive.shape` from two shapes to six and adds `rotation`. They do not conflict semantically, but `AGENTS.md` requires each to be a coordinated change, and they will conflict textually.
- **The two are more separable than they appear.** That branch built its own globe because this one did not exist yet, and its globe cannot draw country or region outlines at all. A workable reconciliation is one globe — this one — with a pluggable click handler: curated locations route to static worlds, and anything else routes to the generation pipeline if the owner wants generation at all. Note that PROJECT_STATUS.md currently lists Grok integration as out of scope, so that is an owner decision, not a merge-time one.

## 7. Responsive Layout

Existing breakpoints in `src/styles/globals.css` (640px, 960px) already handle the world-experience layout reasonably and need no rework. The globe view needs its own treatment, which does not exist yet:

- Full-bleed sizing on mobile. `.globe-stage` currently uses `min-height: calc(100vh - 162px)`, a magic number derived from the desktop header and footer. The header shrinks and the footer stacks on mobile, so the stage overflows there.
- Hover-label and legend placement that does not overlap the globe's touch/drag target.
- Hover is a pointer-only interaction. Decide explicitly what touch devices get, since the region layer is unreachable without hover.

## 8. Accessibility

The card grid this globe replaced was a list of real `<button>` elements — fully keyboard-reachable. The globe is a `<div role="group">` with no keyboard path, so **there is currently no way to select a location without a pointer.** Whatever click design lands in Section 3 needs a parallel keyboard affordance (a visible list of curated destinations alongside the globe is the cheapest option, and doubles as the touch answer above). Track this as a regression to close, not a nice-to-have.

## 9. Testing Plan

Unit coverage for the geo layer (`src/data/geo/geo.test.ts`):

- Country count, and every country code unique and free of `-99` placeholders — the two conditions that would collapse separate countries into one hover target.
- Every pinned country present in the country layer, pin counts and coordinate ranges sane, names unique per country.
- **Every curated `Location` resolves to a real pin.** With baked data this is a build-time guarantee, which is what replaces the runtime fallback the old live-fetch design needed. A `Location` whose city is missing from the bake is silently unreachable.

Playwright coverage in `tests/exploration.spec.ts`:

- Globe intro → morph → interactive globe is reachable, and drag/zoom works.
- Hovering any country shows its outline and label.
- Hovering the US, Japan or Italy reveals pins; a pin stays hoverable while the pointer rests on it; clicking the curated pin enters the era flow and clicking another is a no-op.
- Large countries render their full land area above the sphere, and no feature's fill bleeds across the globe — regression tests for the curvature and winding defects in Section 2. Both are visual failures that unit tests cannot catch; assert them by sampling pixel colours in the interior of the US or Brazil, and in open ocean while a pinned country is hovered. Note that a WebGL canvas needs `preserveDrawingBuffer` for in-page pixel reads, so a Playwright screenshot comparison is the simpler route.

`tests/atmosphere.spec.ts`, `immersive.spec.ts`, `model-loading.spec.ts`, `multi-era.spec.ts` and `quality.spec.ts` all still click the deleted location card and currently fail. They need to be repointed at the Pittsburgh pin, which now works end to end.

Keep the required verification loop from `AGENTS.md`: `npm run format:check`, `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, plus a manual browser pass, and `npm run test:e2e`.
